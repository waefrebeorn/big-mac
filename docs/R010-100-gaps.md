# R010 — 100 Gaps Between Big Mac TTS and Real TTS (research + grounding)

> Big Mac · 2026-08-11 · from the A/B grounding (ours 5.69s vs Apple 3.42s,
> ours garbled) + online research. **The core insight: VocalTractLab
> (Birkholz) proves articulatory synthesis CAN be high-quality and
> intelligible — so the gap is OUR crude implementation, not the approach.**
> Fixing these = closing the intelligibility gap.

## A. COARTICULATION (the #1 fix — why ours snaps instead of glides)
Research: Birkholz 2013 (CV coarticulation via vowel-subspace target
mapping), Cambridge Handbook of Phonetics ch.4 (gesture overlap), Broad &
Clermont target-locus scaling, Stevens landmark theory.

1. No formant-transition smoothing between phone targets — we step-change
   the articulators; speech glides. (THE big one)
2. No anticipatory coarticulation (tongue pre-positions for the next vowel
   during the consonant)
3. No carryover coarticulation (vowel target is never fully reached before
   the next consonant starts — "vowel undershoot")
4. No consonant-dependent vowel target shift (vowel sounds different next
   to /b/ vs /k/)
5. No target-locus scaling (F2 transition locus depends on the following
   vowel — /d/ before /i/ vs /u/ differ)
6. No gestural overlap timing (consonant and vowel gestures phase-shifted,
   not sequential)
7. No context-sensitive consonant shapes (the /s/ tongue shape differs by
   following vowel — Birkholz's vowel-subspace mapping)
8. No syllable-sized coarticulation (articulation planned per syllable,
   not per phone)
9. No stop+stop cluster special handling (first stop's cues are in the
   burst only)
10. No coarticulatory resistance modeling (consonants resist coarticulation
    differently — coronal more than labial)

## B. TIMING / DURATION (ours 5.69s vs real 3.42s = 1.7x too slow)
Research: Klatt 1979 duration rules, van Santen 1993 quantitative duration
modeling, Klatt 1987 review, segmental duration prediction.

11. Phone durations ~1.7x too long (the measured 5.69s vs 3.42s gap)
12. No Klatt-style context duration rules (phone shorter/longer by
    neighboring context)
13. No stress-based duration (stressed vowel longer, but ours uniform)
14. No phrase-final lengthening
15. No prepausal lengthening
16. No consonant-cluster shortening
17. No word-frequency duration effect (common words spoken faster)
18. No speaking-rate normalization (tempo fixed; real speech varies)
19. No voiced/unvoiced duration asymmetry (voiced segments longer)
20. No syllable-timing rhythm (English is stress-timed; ours is flat)
21. Pauses too uniform — no spontaneous pause variation
22. No fillers/hesitation markers
23. No elision/reduction of function words ("and"→"n", "to"→"tə")

## C. PROSODY / INTONATION (rhythm, pitch, emphasis)
Research: prosody = rhythm + pitch + amplitude + timing (NCVS, Respeecher,
PMC2867101); declination; stress; microvariation.

24. No pitch declination (F0 falls gradually across a sentence; ours flat
    within words)
25. No downstepping (each accent lower than the last)
26. No pitch reset between sentences
27. No emphasis/contrast accent (focused word gets extra prominence)
28. No amplitude variation across words (measured: real speech has 30%
    stress amplitude jumps; ours flat)
29. No amplitude declination over the utterance
30. No natural rhythm (stress-timed beat)
31. No microvariation in F0 (cycle-to-cycle; ours deterministic)
32. No microvariation in timing (slight ± on durations)
33. No microvariation in amplitude
34. No sentence-type-dependent contour beyond basic rise/fall
35. No topic/focus structure (given vs new information prosody)
36. No parenthetical/relative-clause intonation
37. No emotion-dependent contour beyond our 6 coarse presets
38. No speech-act contours (statements, requests, exclamations, lists)

## D. SEGMENTAL DETAIL / SOURCE-FILTER (Klatt-level fidelity)
Research: Klatt 1980 cascade/parallel formant synthesizer, KlattGrid
(nasal + 6 frication formants), Childers & Lee 1991 vocal quality,
Dorman 1977 stop bursts.

39. No release burst for stops (the essential /p,t,k,b,d,g/ cue)
40. No VOT (voice onset time) contrast for voiced/voiceless stops
41. No aspiration noise on voiceless stops
42. No fricative formants (we use broadband noise; real /s/ has spectral
    peaks ~4-5 kHz, /sh/ ~2.5 kHz)
43. No frication anti-formants (nasal/obstruent zeroes)
44. No nasal formant + anti-resonance (nasal consonants sound buzzy right)
45. No glottal source spectral tilt control (breathy vs pressed has
    different harmonic rolloff)
46. No breathiness/aspiration mixing (breathy voicing replaces HF harmonics
    with noise — Klatt & Klatt 1990)
47. No vocal fry / creak (common at phrase ends)
48. No diplophonia (two-pulse cycle, rough voices)
49. No whisper mode
50. No lateral /l/ with the right tongue-contact timing
51. No rhotic /r/ with the right F3 lowering (the F3 cue is the main /r/
    identifier)
52. No nasal coarticulation on vowels before nasals
53. No velum timing (opens/closes for nasal sequences)
54. No consonant aspiration at word boundaries
55. No glottal stop /ʔ/ for vowel-initial words
56. No flap/tap /ɾ/ for American /t/ in "better"
57. No schwa reduction control
58. No palatalization of consonants before /i/ (English "cue" vs "coo")
59. No labialization/rounding coarticulation across syllables

## E. ARTICULATORY MODEL (VocalTractLab-level physics)
Research: VocalTractLab (Birkholz) 3D articulatory synthesizer — the
proof articulatory CAN be good; our Pink Trombone is a 1D waveguide.

60. 1D waveguide vs VTL's 3D tract (we can't do velum/rounding properly)
61. No articulator-specific target tracking (tongue body, tip, lips,
    velum move independently)
62. No articulatory effort/undershoot (fast speech reduces target
    attainment)
63. No tongue-tip vs tongue-body distinction (we have one tongue index)
64. No lip rounding as separate from lip aperture
65. No jaw movement (affects F1)
66. No pharyngeal narrowing control
67. No vocal tract length modulation (no /r/-coloring via tract length)
68. No learned articulatory gesture primitives (VTL uses trained
    articulatory target sets)
69. No vocal fold abduction/adduction control (breathy to pressed gradation)
70. No subglottal pressure / loudness coupling (louder = higher F0, real
    voices do this)
71. No fundamental-frequency/intensity coupling (F0 rises with effort)
72. No larynx height change (affects formant structure)

## F. SOURCE (GLOTTAL) MODEL
Research: Childers & Lee 1991 (glottal pulse width, skew, speed quotient),
Klatt & Klatt 1990, R++/LF models.

73. LF model is crude vs Childers' 4-factor glottal model
74. No pulse width/skew/speed-quotient control
75. No glottal formant (the ~3 kHz resonance of the glottal source)
76. No pitch-synchronous spectral tilt change with F0
77. No breathiness-voicing coupling (breathier = noisier glottal source)
78. No vocal tremor (slow F0 oscillation ~4-7 Hz beyond vibrato)
79. No register control (modal vs falsetto vs vocal fry transitions)
80. No phonation onset/offset transients (natural voice "switches on/off"
    smoothly, not abruptly)

## G. INTERACTION / RENDERING
81. No syllable structure (we render phone-by-phone, not CV units)
82. No dynamic F1/F2 tracking for diphthongs (they should glide through
    multiple formant targets)
83. No amplitude envelope shaping per syllable (rise-fall natural)
84. No formant bandwidth variation (narrower for vowels, wider for
    consonants)
85. No spectral detail above F3 (real speech has F4/F5 cues for /r/, /l/)
86. No high-frequency frication detail (4-8 kHz)
87. No stop-burst + formant-transition coupling (they co-vary by vowel)
88. No cross-word resyllabification ("pick it" → "pi-kit")
89. No reduction of unstressed vowels to schwa in connected speech
90. No assimilation ("ten bikes" → "tem bikes")

## H. ANALYSIS/VERIFICATION (measuring the gap)
91. Our analyzer's F0=0.0 lead-in bug hides real pitch stats
92. No formant-tracking over time (we only measure one window; can't
    verify transitions)
93. No MOS/hearing test harness (can't auto-score intelligibility)
94. No phoneme-level accuracy check (can't tell which phones are garbled)
95. No speaking-rate measurement
96. No comparison-to-real-speech regression test

## I. DELIVERY / INTEGRATION
97. No streaming/real-time TTS (renders whole sentence then writes)
98. No SSML/markup (no explicit pause/emphasis control syntax exposed)
99. No multi-voice within a sentence (no narrator/character alternation)
100. No learning loop wired end-to-end (the MLP planner + Q-learner aren't
     yet rewarding from measured intelligibility)

## Priority (the first 5 to build — biggest intelligibility win per effort)
1. **A1+A2+A3: coarticulation smoothing** — interpolate articulators with
   overlap/undershoot (VocalTractLab's core trick). This alone will stop
   the "snapping" = biggest perceived gain.
2. **B11-B13: fix timing** — Klatt duration rules to cut 5.69s → ~3.5s.
3. **D39+D40: stop bursts + VOT** — the essential /p,t,k/ identification cue.
4. **C28-C31: prosodic microvariation** — amplitude/pitch/timing variation.
5. **D42+D44: fricative formants + nasal anti-resonance** — segmental detail.
